# Workflow 1.1

How a book gets made, step by step, and who does each step. Kyle wrote version 1 on 2026-10-19 (`Docs/Sparrow/14 Workflow v1.md`); version 1.1 is the same workflow with the team assigned and two order changes he accepted the same day. The reasons for every call are in `Docs/Sparrow/Research/Team structure 2026-10/5 Ideal team for workflow v1 (2026-10-19).md`. Every agent reads this note before working on a book. The app shows it on the Workflow page.

**How to read the tables.** *Who* names the agents; a lens is a short fresh brief the Story Specialist runs with one skill, blind to the other lenses. *Minds* says whether the step is one mind or several. A *panel* is two to four judges in separate contexts who see the options with names stripped and in both orders (the `judge-panel` skill). Kyle decides at every judging step.

**Three kinds of work.** Generation (brainstorms) gets several different minds. Judgement gets judges who did not build the thing. Integration (one plan, one scene) gets one hand with the whole thing in view.

## Market research

| Step | Who | Minds | Needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Study KindleTrends and similar; identify promising genres | Market Analyst | One | Kyle's screenshots or exports; `market-method` | Reading charts is method work. |
| 2. Competitor and keyword searches on Publisher Rocket | Market Analyst | One | Rocket CSV exports (from the Mac mini) | Mechanical; the Analyst prints headers before reading. |
| 3. Big list of authors selling well; classify them into sub-sub genres | Market Analyst | One, with a blind second pass | Author list, blurbs, titles, subtitles | A misfiled author spreads errors through every later step, so a sample is classified twice in fresh contexts and the agreement rate reported. |
| 4. Recurrent subgenre patterns from titles and subtitles | Market Analyst | One | The classified list | Pattern finding over one table. |
| 5. Identify best opportunities | Market Analyst, then Contrarian | Two | The thesis and the numbers | The Contrarian asks what the predictable book in that niche is and whether Kyle can write it in his voice. |
| 6. Comprehensive genre research: tropes, subtropes, universal fantasies, expectations, plot devices, characters | Market Analyst, with parallel research workers; Story Specialist (genre lens) reviews | Several, in parallel | Comps, blurbs, reviews, Royal Road tags; `genre-primers` | The biggest knowledge step, and parallel work. Ends in one **Genre Bible** note every planning brief reads. Every claim carries its source. |
| 7. Keyword searches from the subgenre research | Market Analyst | One | Rocket | Mechanical. |
| 8. High-value tropes to include | Market Analyst | One | Keyword data, the Genre Bible | Ranked by evidence: search volume and how often they appear in top sellers' blurbs. |
| 9. Report: recommended genre with its highest-value elements | Market Analyst, then Contrarian | Two | Everything above | The report and the Genre Bible are the hand-off to planning. Kyle keeps. |

## Story planning: premise and cast seeds

| Step | Who | Minds | Needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10. Brainstorm premises by Story Juice | Story Specialist runs a room: character, plot, genre and market lenses pitch blind; Contrarian writes its case first | Several (five or six) | Genre Bible, `story-juice` step 2, Taste Profile | Pure generation. |
| 11. A variety of opinions on the Big Issue | Story Specialist, then Contrarian | Two | The kept Big Issue, `story-juice` step 3 | The plain, expected opinions first (Kyle, 2026-10-17), then the ones nobody expects. |
| 12. Backstories that justify the opinions, built on character tropes from the market research | Story Specialist (character lens), one fresh brief per proto-character | Several in parallel, one integrator | The opinion, the Genre Bible's character types, `story-juice` step 4 | Each backstory is independent. The Story Specialist then checks the set does not share one wound, age or job. |

## Pre-writing: test drives

| Step | Who | Minds | Needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13. One-on-one scenes between proto-characters, the ten most compelling pairs | Story Specialist picks the pairs; Writer writes each scene in its own run | One hand per scene, ten runs | Both character notes, a place their plans cross, Style Fingerprint, `story-juice` step 5 | Each scene is prose, so one hand; the ten are independent, so they run at once. |
| 14. Judge which interactions are most interesting | Panel: Developmental Editor, Beta Reader (three personas), Contrarian; Story Specialist sums up | Several (judges) | The ten scenes only, names stripped, both orders | Three kinds of failure: craft, reader appeal, predictability. Kyle keeps. |

## Story planning: main character and supporting cast

Version 1.1 finds the Core Drive and Core Need before the traits, so the trait judge has the drive to judge against.

| Step | Who | Minds | Needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15. Identify the main character | Story Specialist recommends; Contrarian argues for the runner-up | Two | The test-drive verdicts, `story-juice` step 7 | A choice between named options. Kyle decides. |
| 16. Options for the main character's Core Drive | Story Specialist (character lens) runs the six questions; Story Specialist (plot lens) tests each for the scenes it makes; Contrarian names the predictable drive | Three | `story-juice` steps 8 and 9, backstory, kept test drive | The drive grows out of the backstory; the plot lens tests it. |
| 17. Options for the main character's Core Need | Story Specialist | One | `story-juice` step 10, the kept drive | The need is what the drive makes impossible: derived, not brainstormed apart. |
| 18. Twenty traits (two words each), assisted by genre conventions | Story Specialist (character lens, ten; genre lens, ten), blind; Contrarian adds five unlikely ones | Three | Backstory, drive and need, Genre Bible, `character-craft` | Two sources of traits: who this person is, and what the genre's readers expect. |
| 19. Judge the best traits | Developmental Editor; Story Specialist recommends three | One judge, one integrator | The list, backstory, drive, Big Issue | Traits are judged against the story, so flashy ones that fit nothing lose. |
| 20. Twenty distinctive dramatic elements | Story Specialist (character lens and plot lens), blind; Contrarian adds five | Three | As step 18, plus `plot-diagnostics` | A dramatic element is a trait that makes scenes, so the plot lens joins. |
| 21. Judge the best dramatic elements | Developmental Editor; Story Specialist recommends | One judge, one integrator | As step 19 | |
| 22. The most compelling supporting cast, balanced against genre tropes | Story Specialist (genre lens loaded); Market Analyst answers what cast shape the top sellers use | One, with one question out | Genre Bible, the proto-characters, `story-juice` step 11 | Integration: the cast balances against the main character and each other. |
| 23. Each supporting character: Core Drive options, twenty traits, twenty dramatic elements, judge the best | Story Specialist (character lens, one brief per character); Contrarian adds three; Developmental Editor judges | Two generators, one judge | As above | The half-weight version of the main character's steps, to keep cost in proportion. |
| 24. Minor characters from genre conventions | Story Specialist (genre lens) | One | Genre Bible | A list. Minor characters run on an opinion and a want. |
| 25. Conflict opportunities for the main character: external, interpersonal, personal | Story Specialist runs a room: plot lens, character lens and Contrarian pitch blind; Story Specialist fuses | Several (four) | `story-juice` step 12, every kept drive and need, the Big Issue | The pressure system: the three sources must collide, so one hand fuses them. |

## Outlining

| Step | Who | Minds | Needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 26. Ten Save the Cat outlines, character-driven, running on two levels (the main character and the Big Issue; the main character and their Core Drive) | Story Specialist runs eight to ten blind briefs, each a different framing (plot lens from the Big Issue track; plot lens from the drive track; plot lens from the obligatory scenes; character lens from the arc; character lens from the strongest relationship; genre lens from the comps' act breaks); Contrarian writes two, one inverting the obvious ending; Market Analyst writes one from the blurb backwards | Several (eight to ten) | Pressure system, cast, Genre Bible, `save-the-cat`, `story-juice` step 13 | Ten is fine only if no two outlines share a brief and model; ten from one prompt are one outline ten times. Each is one page with both tracks marked at every beat. |
| 27. Judge which outline has the most potential | Panel: Developmental Editor, Beta Reader (three personas, pitch level), Contrarian, Market Analyst; Story Specialist sums up and may enter a fusion of the top two | Several (judges) | The outlines only, names stripped, paired in both orders | Four kinds of failure, four kinds of judge. Kyle decides. |
| 28. Chapter summaries from the winner; each chapter ends with the main character's situation changed by one of the three conflict sources | Story Specialist, in passes: structure, arcs, genre promises, theme | One | Kept outline, pressure system, `chapter-beats`, `serial-chapter-model` | Every chapter depends on the last. Passes keep each prompt short without splitting the hand. |
| 29. Judge the chapter summaries by story principles | Panel: Developmental Editor, Continuity Keeper, Contrarian | Several (three) | The summaries only | Structure, logic and predictability. Notes name the chapter and the fix. |
| 30. Chapter beats with turning points, after Robert McKee | Story Specialist, chapter by chapter | One | Kept summaries, `chapter-beats` with its McKee reference | A turning point changes a value's charge; every chapter's beats name the charge before and after. |
| 31. Judge the chapter beats | Developmental Editor, Continuity Keeper | Two | The beats only | |

## Writing

| Step | Who | Minds | Needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32. Write the novel from the chapter beats | Writer, one scene at a time in story order; Continuity Keeper records after each scene | One hand | Full packet: previous scene and chapter so far in full, the chapter's beats, canon, Style Guide, Style Fingerprint | Sequential and voice-bound. Never parallel Writers. |
| 33. Cold read at the end of Act One | Developmental Editor | One | The Act One chapters only | New in 1.1. A problem visible at chapter eight costs one letter now, not a rewrite later. |
| 34. Check for continuity | Continuity Keeper, full audit | One | All drafted scenes, canon, promises | A judge of work it did not build. |
| 35. Judge the novel for story and writing | Panel: Developmental Editor (story), Beta Reader (three to five personas, one on Opus); then Line Editor (writing) once Kyle accepts the structural notes | Several (judges) | The compiled book only | Story before sentences, or the line edit is thrown away by the rewrite. |
| 36. Rewrite from the critiques | Writer, targeted repairs top rung first, snapshot before each; changed chapters judged again | One hand | The kept notes, the scenes | Repair before redraft. A fresh draft of a chapter only when Kyle asks. |
| 37. Proofread | Line Editor, in a fresh context with the `proofread` skill | One | The final text only | Fresh eyes catch what the line edit missed. |

## What changed from version 1

- Core Drive and Core Need come before the traits and dramatic elements (steps 16 and 17), so traits are judged against the drive.
- A Developmental Editor cold read at the end of Act One (step 33).
- Every step has its team assigned.

## How we judge this workflow

- **Kyle's keeps.** The Taste page shows the keep rate per agent and per brainstorm round. A step whose output Kyle drops most of the time is the first to change.
- **T-032.** The blind test of lenses against the retired specialist agents runs in this workflow's first brainstorms (steps 10, 18 and 25).
- **T-033.** A fresh research round on the team on 2026-12-18.
- **Kyle's notes.** Anything Kyle says about a step goes below, dated, and the next version answers it.
