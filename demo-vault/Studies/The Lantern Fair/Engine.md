## The short answer

- Wren's want (one big score) and her flaw (she works alone) make every scene; Oskar's silence is the world's answer to them.

## What matters about each character

### Wren
- Gift: quick hands
- Cost: nobody to warn her
- Pride: she takes no help
- Pattern: steal, be seen, run the wrong way
- Decoration: the hollow lantern, the prayer she counts in

### Oskar
- Gift: he sees everything
- Cost: he says nothing until it is too late
- Pride: never be blamed
- Pattern: watch, hold back, shout late
- Decoration: the broom, the cart

## What the world does for the plot

- The fair is a crowd: it hides her hands and then closes the lane.

## Why they work

- Each scene turns on a choice made alone; the cost lands on the one who refused help.

## How each scene is built

1. Set up the fair as Wren expects it.
2. Let her plan.
3. Someone sees.
4. She runs; one beat, and out.
